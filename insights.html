Things Worth Exploring.

Small questions, interesting observations, and personal thoughts about where AI meets medical imaging.

Not Everything Needs an Article.

Some ideas are more interesting when you stop, look at them, and simply ask: “What if?”

01

What does AI actually look at in a medical image?

We see anatomy. But what does a model see?

↓

When we look at an X-ray, we may notice bones, lungs, or an area that looks unusual. We understand the image using what we already know about the human body.

An AI model looks at the same image in a different way. It processes the image as data and looks for patterns that it has learned from many examples.

These patterns may help the model decide whether an image looks more like one category or another. But this creates an interesting question: what part of the image is actually influencing its decision?

This is where tools such as Grad-CAM can become interesting. They can help us see which areas of an image contributed to a model's prediction. It does not mean we can see exactly what the AI “thinks”, but it can give us a useful clue about where the model was focusing.
My Thought

When I first started exploring AI in medical imaging, one question kept coming back to me: if a model gives the right answer, how do we know what it actually looked at?

So, when AI looks at a medical image… are we really sure we know what it sees?
02

Does AI understand an image — or recognize patterns?

A simple question with a surprisingly interesting answer.

↓

Imagine showing an AI thousands of ultrasound images labelled as benign or malignant.

During training, the model looks at these examples again and again. Over time, it becomes better at finding patterns that are often connected with the labels it has been given.

But does that mean the AI actually understands what a lesion is? Or has it simply become very good at recognizing patterns that are associated with the answer?

Because a model can sometimes learn something that works very well on its training examples but does not represent the actual medical finding we care about. For example, it might notice a visual pattern that happens to appear often in one group of images. That is why testing AI on new and different images is so important.
My Thought

This is one of the things I find most interesting about medical imaging AI. A correct prediction does not always tell us what the model has learned.

If AI finds the right answer, does it really matter how it got there?
03

Can AI be confident and still be wrong?

Sometimes the number beside a prediction can be misleading.

↓

Imagine an AI system looks at a medical image and says: “Malignant — 99% confidence.”

Sounds convincing, right?

But confidence is not the same thing as correctness. An AI model can be very sure about a prediction and still get it wrong.

This becomes especially important in medical imaging, because an incorrect prediction can affect how an image is reviewed or how a clinical decision is considered.

99%

High confidence does not mean guaranteed correctness.

The number produced by an AI model is based on what the model has learned from its data. If an image is unusual, different from the examples the model learned from, or contains something the model handles poorly, it can still make an incorrect prediction with high confidence. That is why AI should support human expertise rather than replace it.
My Thought

A high percentage can look reassuring. But if AI is going to assist healthcare professionals, I think one of the important questions is not only “How accurate is it?” but also “When is it wrong?”

Would you trust an AI system simply because it says “99% confident”?
