DICOM: The Language That Lets Medical Images “Talk”

How medical imaging systems store, share, and understand imaging information using a common standard.

Imagine this:

A patient gets an MRI in one hospital, then moves to another city for treatment. The new hospital's system opens the scan, along with the information needed to identify and manage the study.

How can different imaging systems make this possible?

That's because of DICOM.

What Is DICOM?

DICOM stands for Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine.

DICOM is a standard that enables medical imaging systems to store, communicate, exchange, and manage imaging information consistently.

In simple words, DICOM gives medical imaging systems a common language.

A CT scan from a Siemens machine. An X-ray from a GE machine. An ultrasound from a Philips. Different brands, different software, and different hospitals can exchange imaging information when the systems involved support the DICOM standard.

Why Do We Need DICOM?

A medical image is not just about what appears on the screen.

When a radiologist reviews an imaging study, there is important information associated with it:

  • Who is the patient?
  • What examination was performed?
  • Which body region was examined?
  • When was the study performed?
  • Which modality produced the image?
  • What technical information is associated with the image?

DICOM provides a standardized structure for associating this information with imaging data.

Medical image + structured information + standardized communication

This makes imaging information easier for compatible systems to identify, organize, exchange, and process.

What Is Inside a DICOM File?

A DICOM object can contain the actual image data together with associated information about the patient, study, series, modality, and image or acquisition details. The exact information fields depend on the type of DICOM object.

1. Pixel Data (The Image):

The actual visual medical image, such as an X-ray, CT image, or MRI image, represented as digital pixel data.

2. Associated Information (Metadata):

Information related to the patient, examination, imaging modality, date and time, and technical details of the image or acquisition.

This additional information is often called metadata.

What Are DICOM Tags?

DICOM organizes information into small fields called tags.

For example:

DICOM Tag Example Information
(0010,0010) Patient Name
(0010,0020) Patient ID
(0008,0060) Modality

You do not need to memorize these numbers to understand the idea.

Think of DICOM tags like labels on different boxes. One label identifies the patient, another identifies the examination, and another identifies the imaging modality.

How Does DICOM Work in Real Life?

A simplified imaging workflow looks like this:

01 Scanner CT / MRI / X-ray
→
02 DICOM Data Image + Information
→
03 Hospital System Imaging data
→
04 Viewer Image review
→
05 Radiologist Clinical review

For example:

Imagine a trauma patient arrives in the emergency department. A CT scan is taken. The imaging data can then be sent from the CT system to the hospital's PACS and made available on the radiologist's workstation, or potentially to another authorized location for consultation.

This workflow can happen because the systems involved can communicate using DICOM.

Now imagine if every manufacturer used its own completely incompatible format instead. Emergency care could become slower, second opinions could be harder to arrange, and sharing imaging information between hospitals would become much more difficult.

A PACS can also be part of this workflow. DICOM and PACS are not the same thing—this difference will be explained separately.

DICOM vs JPG or PNG

A JPG or PNG file can show an image very well, but it is not designed specifically for medical imaging workflows.

JPG / PNG DICOM
General-purpose image format Medical imaging standard
Mainly stores the image Can store image data with associated information
Used for everyday images Designed for medical imaging systems
Not designed for full medical imaging workflows Supports medical imaging workflows and communication

For example, a screenshot of a CT image saved as JPG may show the anatomy, but it does not provide the same structured medical information and workflow support as the original DICOM data.

Why Is DICOM Important?

Imagine a hospital using scanners from several different manufacturers.

Without a common standard, exchanging medical imaging information between different systems could become much more difficult.

DICOM helps different medical imaging systems communicate and work together.

This makes it possible to move imaging information through modern healthcare environments—from acquisition and storage to viewing and clinical use.

The Big Picture

DICOM is more than a file extension.

It is a common standard for medical imaging information that helps images and their associated information move between compatible systems.

So, when medical imaging information needs to travel from one system to another, DICOM helps make sure that compatible systems can interpret the information being exchanged.

Key Takeaway

DICOM gives medical imaging systems a common language.

It helps keep medical images connected with important examination information and allows different compatible systems to exchange and understand that information.

In simple terms:

DICOM helps medical images “talk” to different systems.
